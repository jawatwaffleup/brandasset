# WaffleUp Social Engine

**Version 1.0 · 23 August 2026 · Owner: Marketing**
**Objective it serves:** frequency (primary) · consistent social presence (standing)

---

## 0. What this is, and why it isn't a campaign

A campaign gives you three weeks of posts and then an empty calendar. This is a **system**: eight named, repeatable formats you can run indefinitely without writing a new brief each time.

Each format below carries its own layout, copy pattern, production route, Higgsfield prompt template and success metric. Pick a format, drop in this week's product, ship it.

**Deliberately product-agnostic.** No format is built around a specific SKU. Rotate the range. Concentrate on one product only for a launch, and only when asked.

### The three rules that govern everything here

1. **70/30.** Roughly seven in ten posts carry **no CTA at all** and are judged on reach, saves and shares — never on orders. This is agreed, and it must hold when a quiet month arrives. Judging brand-heat posts on order volume kills the system by week six.
2. **Never say "free" or "discount."** Never quantify a giveaway. Full rule in `CLAUDE.md` §3. If the customer could work out the taka value from the words on screen, rewrite it.
3. **Characters appear, they don't speak.** Supplied artwork only, never AI-generated, never redrawn, **no first-person dialogue** until `brand/character-voices/` is written and approved.

## 2. The formats

### 1 · THE DROP

**Job.** Pure craving. The house product shot, done properly, on a rotating SKU.

**Looks like.** The established house recipe from `assets/marketing/product-hero/`: product levitating mid-frame, two-tone colour-blocked backdrop, hard directional light with a crisp shadow, ingredients frozen in the air, branded box slightly out of focus behind.

**Spec.** 1080×1350 (4:5) feed · or 1080×1920 for Stories/Reels · saturated, clean, no clutter.

**Copy pattern.** One line. Sensory, a little over the top. Product name does the work.

- "Tri-Chocolate. Three problems, solved."
- "Mad Mango. Named by someone who was there."
- "Very Very Strawberry. The name took four seconds."
- "Kunaffle™. Here for a limited time, gone for a long one."

**Metric.** Saves. A save is a craving deferred, which is exactly the behaviour this format exists to create.

**Higgsfield prompt template — background plate only:**

```
Studio product backdrop, no product present. Two-tone colour block:
upper two-thirds a flat wall in warm honey gold, lower third a pale
seamless surface in soft mint. Hard directional key light from upper
left casting one crisp, well-defined shadow across the surface.
Saturated, clean, high contrast, no texture noise. Shot on 50mm,
f/8, straight-on, eye level. Vertical 4:5.

Negative: no product, no food, no waffle, no logo, no text, no
lettering, no brand marks, no cartoon characters, no people, no hands,
no props, no soft diffuse lighting, no grey or beige tones, no
gradient backdrop, no watermark, no clutter.
```

> **Compositing:** the product is **always** a real photograph from `assets/marketing/product-hero/` or a real shoot, cut out and layered onto this plate. AI must never render the waffle — the grid, the stick and the crumb come out wrong every time and it is the one thing the audience knows by heart. Add falling ingredients as separate real cut-outs or practical elements.

### 2 · SOUND ON

**Job.** The anti-soggy proof, and the sharpest weapon you have. This format attacks the stated Pain directly: dessert that travels to you and arrives dead.

**Looks like.** Extreme macro. A bite, a snap, a break. Shallow depth. The sound is the content — the picture is almost secondary.

**Spec.** 1080×1920, 30fps, 5–9 seconds, **sound-on by design**, no music bed over the crunch. Caption card in frame for muted autoplay.

**Copy pattern.** Short. Let the audio carry it.

- "Volume up."
- "That's the whole post."
- "Squares don't go soft."
- Bangla: "আওয়াজটা শুনুন।"

**Metric.** Watch-through rate and sound-on rate. Shares if it lands.

**Production — be honest about this one.** ❌ **Higgsfield cannot make this.** There is no AI route to a real crunch: the sound has to be real, the crumb has to be real, and the audience can tell instantly. This format needs a phone, a macro lens or clip-on, a quiet room and ten minutes. Shoot six of them in one session and you have two months of this format banked.

**Shooting note.** Record in a quiet room, not an outlet. Hard side light, dark background, product close to the mic. The crunch is the asset — protect it.

### 3 · ON THE MOVE

**Job.** "Joy that travels," made literal. The stick is a handle; the square packs flat. This is the promise as a picture.

**Looks like.** Product in hand, in motion. Walking, on a rickshaw, in a car, crossing a street, in a queue. Never sitting at a table — the whole point is that no table is required.

**Spec.** 1080×1920, 30fps, 6–12s. Handheld, slightly imperfect. Should feel filmed, not produced.

**Copy pattern.**

- "No table. No fork. No plan."
- "Dessert with a handle."
- "Built for the walk home."
- Bangla: "হাতে নিন, হাঁটতে থাকুন।"

**Metric.** Shares. This is the format that explains the brand to someone who doesn't know it.

**Higgsfield prompt template — street plate:**

```
Handheld POV walking shot moving forward along a busy urban street at
dusk, South Asian city, warm streetlights beginning to glow, shop
signage bokeh, light foot traffic, motion blur at the edges. Camera at
chest height, natural handheld sway, 28mm wide. Hard mixed lighting,
high contrast, saturated. Colour grade pushed toward electric cyan and
hot bubblegum pink in the highlights and signage. 9:16 vertical, 30fps, 8
seconds.

Negative: no food, no product, no dessert, no logo, no text, no
lettering, no brand marks, no cartoon characters, no recognisable faces,
no soft diffuse lighting, no grey or beige palette, no watermark, no
drone or aerial shot, no slow motion.
```

> **Compositing:** film the real hand and real product against a simple background, then layer over the AI street plate. Or simplest and often best — **just shoot it on the street.** The AI plate is for when weather, permission or time don't allow.

### 4 · NOT-SO-FACTS

**Job.** Brand personality at near-zero production cost, extending the single funniest thing WaffleUp already owns.

**Looks like.** Your packaging already carries a "Not-So-Nutrition Facts" panel — *Joy: infinite · Regret: 0g · Guilt: removed · Self-control: -100% · Crunch Factor: loud enough to wake neighbours*. That deadpan register is the brand's tone bullseye. This format lifts it onto social as a weekly typographic post.

**Spec.** 1080×1350. Typography only. Brand pattern or flat colour field. Use the supplied word-marks in `assets/typography/` for any hero word rather than rebuilding the four-layer effect.

**Copy pattern.** Straight-faced nutrition-panel parody. Never explain the joke. Product-agnostic by design.

```
SERVING SIZE          One. Technically.
CALORIES COUNTED      No.
REGRET                0g
DECISIONS QUESTIONED  1
CRUNCH                Audible from the next room
CONTAINS              Nuts, joy, and a stick
```

Single-line variants for Stories:
- "Servings per box: one happy soul."
- "Who's asking? — the calorie section, probably."

**Metric.** Saves and shares. Deadpan travels further than jokes that try.

**Production.** No AI needed. Type, brand pattern, done. **This is your cheapest recurring slot — never skip it in a busy week.**

### 5 · GANG SIGHTING

**Job.** Keep the character universe alive between Surprise Card seasons, and build the recognition the collection mechanic will later depend on.

**Looks like.** One character — supplied artwork, full-colour, unmodified — placed in a real or generated environment. The character observes, reacts or simply *is there*. No speech.

**Spec.** 1080×1350 or 1080×1920. One character per post, never a crowd.

**Copy pattern.** The caption narrates; the character never talks.

- "Bhoppu found the high ground again."
- "Tvy has thoughts about the new flavour. Tvy always has thoughts."
- "Spacy has not been briefed on closing time."

**Metric.** Comments. Characters generate replies in a way product shots don't.

**Constraint — read before writing.** Supplied art only, from `assets/characters/`. Never AI-generated, never redrawn, never recoloured, never a new pose. **No first-person dialogue** until character voices are approved. If the pose you want doesn't exist, change the idea — don't fabricate the pose.

**Higgsfield prompt template — environment only:**

```
Empty urban environment plate, South Asian city at golden hour, a low
concrete ledge in the foreground with clean empty space, softly blurred
street behind. Hard directional sunlight from the left, strong crisp
shadows, saturated warm tones. Composition leaves the lower-right third
completely empty and unobstructed. 35mm, eye level, 4:5 vertical.

Negative: no characters, no people, no animals, no cartoon, no mascot,
no food, no product, no logo, no text, no lettering, no brand marks,
no soft lighting, no grey or beige palette, no watermark.
```

> **Compositing:** the character PNG drops into the empty third. The prompt is written to leave that space clear — keep that instruction in any variant you write.

### 6 · CAUGHT CRISPY

**Job.** Social proof, and the cheapest content you will ever run. Other people's photos.

**Looks like.** A customer's own photo or video, reposted with credit and a light frame — brand pattern edge or a small plaque. Minimal intervention; over-designing it destroys the credibility that makes it work.

**Spec.** Whatever they shot. Story reposts and feed carousels both work.

**Copy pattern.** Short, warm, credit them.

- "@handle found the good light."
- "Someone's evening is going better than ours."
- "Reposting this until they ask us to stop."

**Metric.** Volume of inbound tags. If tags rise month over month, the flywheel is turning.

**How to create supply.** UGC doesn't just arrive; it needs a reason and a route.
1. Ask, occasionally and lightly, in Stories: *"Tag us. We're nosy."*
2. Repost consistently — people post when they believe they'll be seen.
3. **Surprise Card completions are the highest-value UGC you'll get** once seasons launch (see `programs/SURPRISE-CARD-GUIDELINE.md` §9). A finished set is inherently postable, and the prize is the content.

**Permission.** Always ask before reposting to feed. Story reposts of a tag are normal practice; a feed post is republishing someone's work and needs a yes.

### 7 · THE LATE SHIFT

**Job.** Claim the evening. Your rush is evening through late night, three of your ten characters have "late night" written into their approved bios, and no competitor owns that slot in Dhaka. This format plants the flag.

**Looks like.** Night. City lights, neon, the glow of a window-format outlet. Product present but not hero — the *time* is the hero.

**Spec.** 1080×1920. **Post it in the window** — 8–11pm local. A late-night post at 2pm is a different post.

**Copy pattern.** Time-stamped, deadpan, no hard sell.

- "10:40pm. Still square."
- "The kitchen closes. Eventually."
- "Dinner was three hours ago. That's basically ancient history."
- Bangla: "রাত হয়েছে। তাতে কী?"

**Metric.** Engagement rate *within* the 8–11pm window specifically. Compare against your daytime posts — if it doesn't beat them, the format isn't earning its slot.

**⚠ [CONFIRM]** Actual closing hours per outlet type before any post states or implies a time. Chef's Table, standard outlets and cloud kitchens will differ.

**Higgsfield prompt template — night plate:**

```
Night street scene in a South Asian city, wet asphalt reflecting neon
signage, warm shop-window glow, deep shadows, a few blurred figures in
the far background. Hard practical lighting, very high contrast,
saturated. Electric cyan and hot bubblegum pink dominate the reflections and
signage glow; deep cocoa-brown shadows, never pure black. 35mm, low
angle, 9:16 vertical, 30fps, 6 seconds.

Negative: no food, no product, no logo, no text, no lettering, no brand
marks, no cartoon characters, no recognisable faces, no daylight, no
soft diffuse lighting, no grey or beige palette, no watermark, no rain
falling, no drone shot.
```

### 8 · ORDER UP

**Job.** The explicit direct-response slot. Because this format exists and carries the CTA load, the other seven don't have to — which is what protects the 70/30 split from quietly drifting to 30/70.

**Looks like.** Clean, direct, product-forward. Less atmosphere, more clarity.

**Spec.** 1:1 or 4:5 for feed; 1:1 for delivery-platform tiles where the product must fill the frame with no small text.

**Copy pattern.** Direct without ever naming value. **No "free", no "discount", no price framing.**

- "Foodpanda. Pathao. Or the short walk."
- "It's on the app. It's also four minutes away."
- "Open now. Boxes are stacked."
- Bangla: "অর্ডার করুন — ফুডপ্যান্ডা, পাঠাও, অথবা সরাসরি।"

**Metric.** This is the only format judged on orders. Link clicks, platform traffic, direct enquiries.

**⚠ Channel rule — check before every ORDER UP post.** **Chef's Table has no delivery.** Never attach a Foodpanda or Pathao CTA to Chef's Table content. And per `brand/BRAND.md`, all channels are live and parallel — Foodpanda, Pathao, Foodi, BuyHereNow, own delivery, WhatsApp and walk-in. Route to whichever is nearest to hand, not to one preferred surface.

## 4. The first month

Five posts a week, twenty a month. **Six carry a CTA** (marked ⚑) — that's the 30%.

Days shown are Bangladesh. For Singapore, shift the anchor to Sat/Sun and add the Wednesday slot.

| Week | Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri | Sat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | NOT-SO-FACTS | — | SOUND ON | THE DROP | ON THE MOVE ⚑ | CAUGHT CRISPY |
| **2** | GANG SIGHTING | — | THE DROP | SOUND ON | THE LATE SHIFT ⚑ | ORDER UP ⚑ |
| **3** | NOT-SO-FACTS | — | CAUGHT CRISPY | THE DROP ⚑ | ON THE MOVE | SOUND ON |
| **4** | GANG SIGHTING | — | THE LATE SHIFT | CAUGHT CRISPY | THE DROP ⚑ | ORDER UP ⚑ |

**Why it's shaped like this:**
- **CTAs cluster Thu–Sat**, into the peak, where intent is already highest. Capacity has headroom, so there's no penalty for pushing the peak.
- **Mondays are the cheapest slots** — NOT-SO-FACTS and GANG SIGHTING need no shoot. A busy week never breaks the calendar.
- **Tuesday is deliberately empty.** Five well-made posts beat seven rushed ones, and the gap is your buffer for anything reactive.
- **LATE SHIFT posts go out 8–11pm.** Everything else, evening where possible.
- **No product is repeated in consecutive DROP slots.** Rotate the range.

## 5. Running it

**Weekly, ~90 minutes:**
1. Pick next week's five formats from the calendar.
2. Choose the product for each DROP — rotate, don't repeat.
3. Pull assets: real photography for product, `assets/characters/` for GANG, `assets/typography/` for hero words.
4. Generate any AI plates needed. Composite.
5. Write captions against the format's copy pattern.
6. Schedule. LATE SHIFT into the 8–11pm window.

**Monthly, batched — this is what makes it survivable:**
- Shoot **six SOUND ON clips in one session.** Two months banked in twenty minutes.
- Write **four NOT-SO-FACTS panels** at once. They're the same joke in different clothes.
- Collect UGC as it arrives; don't hunt for it on the day.

**Monthly review — the only two questions that matter:**
1. Did the brand-heat posts grow reach, saves and shares?
2. Did the ⚑ posts move orders?

They are different questions with different answers. **Never merge them into one number**, and never apply question 2 to a post that was built to answer question 1.
