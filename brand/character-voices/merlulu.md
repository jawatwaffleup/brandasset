## 1. Voice in one sentence
The one who makes sure everyone's actually having a good time.

## 2. Personality and energy
Trendy, loyal, energetic, social. Sporty and competitive, but the competition is a reason to be together rather than a reason to win. Warm, easy company. Bold without being loud — the "boldly playful" in the bio is about **confidence**, not volume.
**Energy 4/5 · Warmth 4/5**
**Interests:** gaming, fitness, sport, parties, music.

## 3. Vocabulary, catchphrases, language mix
- **Plural by default:** we, us, everyone, come on, who else.
- Invitations rather than announcements. Most lines include someone else.
- Gaming and sport framing — rounds, teams, rematch, next one.
- Warm, quick, unfussy. Not clever for its own sake.
- **English-led.** Bangla is now permitted where the placement calls for it — short, warm and plain, never a laboured pun. He is not a Bangla-first voice.
- No approved catchphrase. **Don't invent one.**

## 4. May / may not
**May:** invite, gather, compete for fun, be loyal, be social, carry Singapore heritage lightly.
**May not:** be timid · be sarcastic · make the Merlion/heritage point heavy or educational · quote a price, outlet or delivery CTA for the wrong market. **The roster is universal; the market facts are not** — see `CLAUDE.md` §2.

## 7. Nearest neighbour
**Air Maxi.** The tightest collision in the cast — both sporty, headphoned, party-coded, pink-and-gold, and the brief openly says they share qualities. The split:

> **Air Maxi chases a standard. Merlulu builds a group.**

**Neither is selfish and neither is rude** — that is not the difference. Air Maxi's energy points at **the goal** and pulls people along behind it; Merlulu's points at **the people**, and the activity is the excuse. Air Maxi says *go further*. Merlulu says *come along*. If a line works for both, it isn't finished. **Market separation used to keep them apart. It no longer does** — since 24 Aug 2026 they can share a frame, so this distinction now does all the work on its own.
