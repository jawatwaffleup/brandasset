**Status: DRAFT — awaiting approval · Rebuilt 24 Aug 2026**
Character source of truth: **`brand/CHARACTER-BIBLE.md`.** This file defers to it.

Read this before writing any character line. Individual voice files describe a character; **this file is what stops them collapsing into each other.**

## The three collapse risks

**1 · Air Maxi vs Merlulu — the tightest.** Both sporty, headphoned, party-coded, pink-and-gold. The written brief openly admits the overlap. **Market separation no longer does any work** — the roster unified on 24 Aug 2026, so they can appear together at any time. Only this line separates them:

> **Air Maxi chases a standard. Merlulu builds a group.**

**Neither is selfish and neither is rude.** *(Corrected by Jawat, 24 Aug 2026 — an earlier draft framed Air Maxi as possessive, which was wrong.)* Air Maxi's energy points at **the goal** and pulls people along; Merlulu's points at **the people**, and the activity is the excuse. Air Maxi says *go further*. Merlulu says *come along*.

**2 · Picchi vs Spacy — the same person.** They *should* feel related; they must not feel interchangeable. Spacy has **been there**. If a Spacy line is a question with no experience behind it, it's Picchi's.

**3 · Bhoppu vs Icy vs Picchi — the warm-hyper cluster.** All three read "energetic and lovable." Split by **what the energy is aimed at**:

> **Bhoppu = the reward · Picchi = the discovery · Icy = the next place**

Same warmth, three different targets. If a line would work for two of them, it isn't finished.

## Movement, not just voice

New in this version — from `CHARACTER-BIBLE.md`. Three movement languages, no overlap:

> **Bhoppu = mass · Air Maxi = motion · Mr Waffle = stillness**

Everyone else sits between them. This binds visual briefs the way the voice separations bind copy. If two characters move the same way in a frame, one of them is wrong.

**Hard movement rules:** Spacy is **never on the ground** · Mr Waffle **floats** (R2-D2 reference) · Swirly **bounces and swirls**, never walks.

## Reading the audition

Every voice file uses the same six situations. **Read across characters, not down.** Cover the names — if you can't tell who's speaking, the voices aren't distinct enough yet.
