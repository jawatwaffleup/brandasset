## 1. The strategic read — you buried the lede

You described WaffleUp to me for three weeks as a waffle chain. Then, in one sentence, you mentioned you have **a shelf-stable premix that makes a waffle by adding water, and the waffle stays crunchy for over an hour.**

That is not a footnote. **That is the company.**

Here is why the distinction is worth roughly everything in a fundraise:

| Framed as a waffle chain | Framed as what it actually is |
|---|---|
| 15 dessert outlets in Dhaka | A food format that removed the cold chain from dessert |
| Revenue = footfall × ticket | Revenue = premix volume × franchisee count × royalty |
| Growth = capex per new site | Growth = capital-light, franchisee-funded |
| Moat = brand and location | Moat = **formulation IP + a franchise system built on it** |
| Comparable: a regional QSR chain | Comparable: the franchisors who sell their franchisees the mix |
| Valuation: revenue multiple, low | Valuation: IP + recurring B2B supply + franchise royalty |
| Geographic ceiling: Bangladesh | Ceiling: anywhere you can ship a dry bag |

A USD 1M cheque into a 15-site dessert chain in Dhaka is a hard sell to almost any investor. Restaurant chains are capital-hungry, margin-thin, operationally local and historically poor venture returns.

A USD 1M cheque into **a proprietary premix business with 15 company-owned outlets already proving demand at ~8,000 units a day** is a completely different conversation, with a different investor set and a different multiple.

**The 15 outlets are not the business you are selling. They are the proof that the business works.** They are your R&D lab, your demand validation, your training academy and your showroom. That reframe is the single most valuable thing in this document.

### The four claims, and what they are actually worth

You named four edges. Each is genuinely strong — and each is currently **an assertion, not an asset.** Converting them is the pre-raise work.

**1. "We made the waffle premix — just add water."**
This is the wedge. Its real value is not convenience, it is what it *deletes*:
- No eggs, no milk, no butter at the outlet → **no refrigeration at the point of sale**
- Every outlet produces an identical waffle → solves the number-one franchise failure mode
- Training drops from weeks to minutes → lower labour cost, faster openings, lower staff-churn damage
- Fewer SKUs, longer shelf life, less spoilage, simpler logistics
- It is dry goods → **it ships, it exports, it clears customs easily**

**2. "Crunchy for more than an hour."**
This is the delivery unlock and it is the technical heart of the pitch. Most waffles surrender in fifteen minutes. An hour of structural crunch expands the viable delivery radius, cuts complaint and refund rates, and is the literal engineering proof of the brand's own promise — *joy that travels*, *arrives crisp*. **If it is lab-verified, it is a moat. If it is a founder's opinion, it is marketing copy an investor will discount to zero.**

**3. "Franchise ready."**
Only true when a stranger with money can buy a unit, follow a manual and make the same waffle. Claims 1 and 2 are what make it *possible*. The documentation is what makes it *real*, and it does not exist yet.

**4. "Easily profitable — not cold-chain dependent."**
The strongest line in the whole pitch **and the most dangerous**, because "easily profitable" is a claim an investor will test within ten minutes of diligence. In Bangladesh, no cold chain also means resilience to load-shedding and grid instability — say that explicitly, it is a real operational advantage that a Gulf or South-East Asian investor will immediately understand.

### The story in one line

> **We took the cold chain out of dessert. A dry premix, water, ninety seconds — and a waffle that is still crunchy an hour later. We proved it across 15 sites and ~8,000 units a day. Now we are selling the system, not the shop.**

## 2. The business model to pitch — three lines, one flywheel

Do not pitch one revenue line. Pitch three, and show how they compound.

1. **Premix manufacturing & supply** — B2B, recurring, high gross margin, exportable. *The investable asset.*
2. **Franchise fees + royalties** — capital-light expansion funded by franchisees. *The multiplier.*
3. **Company-owned outlets** — cash flow, brand control, and the lab where new products are proven before they enter the system. *The proof layer.*

**The flywheel, and it must be one slide:**

> Premix removes the cold chain and guarantees consistency → franchising becomes cheap, fast and low-risk → more franchisees open → premix volume rises → unit cost falls and R&D budget grows → the product gets better and the range widens → franchising becomes more attractive still.

Owned outlets sit in the middle: every new product is tested there first, and only enters the premix system once it works.

**This is a proven model, not an invention.** The strongest franchise businesses in food make their money selling franchisees a proprietary mix, not on franchise fees. Name that pattern in the room — investors recognise it instantly, and it moves you out of the "restaurant" mental bucket.

## 4. Who to raise from

USD 1M is a seed ticket. In Bangladesh it is a substantial one. You will most likely assemble it from **two or three cheques plus a strategic**, not one lead.

Four pools, in order of how well they fit the premix story:

**a) Regional and frontier-market VC.** The best fit, because the premix reframe reads as food-tech rather than F&B. Frontier-focused funds and South-East Asian early-stage funds understand franchise-plus-supply models and can carry you into their portfolio markets.

**b) Bangladesh institutional VC and the government vehicle.** Local capital knows the market, the regulatory path and the FX reality, and validates you domestically. Typically smaller cheques — useful for filling a round, rarely for leading a repositioned one.

**c) Strategic — and this is the underrated one.** Two kinds:
- **Ingredient and milling groups.** Your premix needs a manufacturing partner. A strategic investor who is also your production partner de-risks the plant line item and brings food-science capability you would otherwise hire.
- **GCC franchise operators.** In the Gulf, the party that invests is often the same party that takes the master franchise. That converts your Dubai line item from a cost into a funded, partner-operated expansion. **For a franchise-led business, this is frequently the highest-value money on the table.**

**d) Family offices and angels, Dhaka and diaspora.** Fast, relationship-driven, less process. Good for closing the tail of a round. Weak on follow-on and signalling.

> **Every name in these categories must be qualified before contact** — current mandate, cheque size, stage, sector, whether they have deployed in Bangladesh, and whether they can hold a position in a food business. Fund mandates shift constantly and I have no verified current data on any of them. Building and qualifying that list is a named task in `18-MONTH-PLAN.md` §1.

### Two structural facts that will come up in every single meeting

**Waffle Up Global PTE Ltd is a genuine asset — use it.** A Singapore holding company is the structure foreign investors already know how to invest into: familiar law, clean share transfers, workable exits. **Confirm the Bangladesh operating company is a properly documented subsidiary of it**, and deal with the third entity. If that chain is clean, you have removed the biggest single obstacle to foreign capital entering a Bangladeshi business.

**FX and repatriation is the elephant, and you must raise it first.** Foreign investors into Bangladesh worry about BDT devaluation, dollar availability and getting profits and exit proceeds out. Do not wait to be asked. Address it head-on with the structure, and note the natural hedge you are building: **premix export earns foreign currency, and international franchise royalties are collected offshore at the Singapore level.** That is a real answer, not a deflection — and it is another reason the premix reframe is the right one.

## 5. Valuation and instrument — how to think, not what to say

I will not put a number on this and neither should you until the financials reconcile. What to hold in your head:

- USD 1M at typical seed dilution of **15–25%** implies a post-money somewhere around **USD 4–6.7M**. That is arithmetic, not a valuation.
- **The framing moves the number more than the numbers do.** The same business pitched as a dessert chain and as a premix-and-franchise platform will not be valued within twice each other.
- Your defensible inputs will be reconciled revenue and outlet-level EBITDA, premix gross margin, franchise pipeline, and the IP position. **Three of those four do not exist yet.**
- **Instrument:** a priced round demands a valuation you cannot yet defend. A convertible instrument at the Singapore level, with a cap, defers the valuation fight until you have the numbers — often the right call when a repositioning is still being proven. Take real counsel on this; it is a legal and tax question in two jurisdictions, not a marketing one.
- **Do not over-dilute at seed.** A franchise-and-supply business scales on franchisee capital. If the model works, this should be the most expensive money you ever take.

## 6. Sequencing — and one efficiency worth real money

You said the profile comes first, then the deck. Right order — but they overlap more than you think, and one decision will save you a five-figure sum.

**The photo shoot must be extended now, before it happens.** `company-profile/CAPTURE-BRIEF.md` covers the estate, product, team and packaging. A premix-led investor deck needs material that brief barely touches:

- **The premix itself** — the bag, the powder, the pour, the branding
- **Production** — mixing, filling, sealing, QC, warehouse, batch coding
- **The lab** — testing in progress, the texture analyser, the shelf-life rack
- **The crunch demonstration** — the hour-long test, filmed as evidence, not as an ad
- **Franchise proof** — an outlet being built, a training session, a franchisee at work
- **Founder for an investor audience** — a different register from the profile portrait

Shooting this twice costs double and delays the raise. **Add a premix and production block to the capture brief before anyone picks up a camera.** That is the single highest-return action available this month, and it costs nothing today.

**What else the two documents share:** the estate register, the verified 2026 numbers, the awards and certification pack, the press archive, and the entity structure. Gather each once. `company-profile/GATHER.md` already owns that list — the fundraise simply raises the bar on every item from *publishable* to *diligence-grade*.

**What they must never share is tone.** The profile is warm and Jester-inflected. The deck is plain, fast and numerate. Same facts, different register. A pitch deck that tries to be funny about its own unit economics loses the room.

## 7. The honest risks — name them before they are found

Investors trust founders who volunteer the weak points. Put these in the deck, with a mitigation each.

| Risk | The honest answer |
|---|---|
| **Single-market concentration** | 14 of 15 sites in one country. Mitigation is the whole plan: premix export and international franchise |
| **FX, devaluation, repatriation** | Singapore holdco, offshore royalty collection, export earnings as a natural hedge |
| **Founder dependency** | Real. Mitigation is the readiness sprint's key hires and the documented franchise system |
| **Commodity exposure** | Cocoa, chocolate, dairy and flour have all moved hard. Premix scale improves buying power; forward contracts are the answer |
| **Franchise quality control** | Historically what kills franchise brands. **Your premix is the answer** — consistency is manufactured upstream, not policed downstream. Make this a strength slide, not a risk slide |
| **The IP may be weaker than hoped** | A formulation may be hard to patent. Trade-secret regime plus brand plus system plus scale is a legitimate stack — but know which one you are actually relying on before you are asked |
| **Regulatory and halal across markets** | Certification per market is a cost and a timeline, not a blocker. Budget it |
