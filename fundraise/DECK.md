# The pitch deck — slide by slide

**16 slides plus an appendix.** 16:9, built through `brand-guidelines/build.py` like every other WaffleUp document.

Three rules that govern every slide:

1. **The premix appears by slide 4.** Not slide 11. An investor decides what kind of company you are in the first two minutes, and "dessert chain" is the wrong answer.
2. **One claim per slide, one proof per claim.** If a slide asserts something the appendix cannot evidence, cut the slide or get the evidence.
3. **The register is plain, fast and numerate.** The Jester voice belongs in the company profile. Here it costs you credibility. *Confident and dry, never cute.*

> **Every number below is a placeholder.** Nothing goes on a slide until it comes from the reconciled source in `DILIGENCE.md`.

---

## The arc

**Slides 1–5** — you are not what they assumed. **6–9** — it works, and here are the numbers. **10–13** — it scales, and here is how. **14–16** — these are the people, this is the money, this is the size of it.

## Part 1 — The reframe (slides 1–5)

### 1 · Cover
Logo, one line, the ask, the date.

The line is the whole thesis compressed: **"We took the cold chain out of dessert."** Then, smaller: *WaffleUp Global PTE Ltd · raising USD 1M seed.*

Full-bleed photograph — the premix bag and a finished waffle in one frame. Not a shop.

### 2 · The problem
Dessert has always been an occasion you travelled to and ate at a table. When it finally started travelling to *you*, it arrived soggy, melted and obviously built for delivery. And every dessert business that tried to scale hit the same wall: **cold chain**. Refrigeration at every site, spoilage, inconsistency between outlets, and capex that makes each new unit expensive.

*Why this slide works:* it opens as a consumer problem and closes as an operator problem. The second half is what an investor is actually buying.

**Proof needed:** none. This is the setup.

### 3 · What we built
The product, plainly. The stick is a handle. The square packs flat and does not crush. Ninety seconds from dry powder to a finished waffle.

Photography, not copy. Let the product carry it.

**Proof needed:** the product photography, documentary treatment — real hand, real street.

### 4 · **The premix — the wedge**
**The most important slide in the deck.** Everything before it is setup; everything after it is consequence.

A dry, shelf-stable premix. Add water. Ninety seconds. And what that deletes:

| What goes away | What it means |
|---|---|
| Eggs, milk, butter at the outlet | **No refrigeration at point of sale** |
| Variation between outlets | Every waffle identical, everywhere |
| Weeks of training | Minutes. Faster openings, lower labour cost |
| Spoilage and complex inventory | Dry goods, long shelf life, simple logistics |
| Cold-chain logistics | **It ships. It exports. It clears customs.** |

Close on the second claim: **the waffle is still crunchy an hour later** — with the lab number, not the adjective.

**Proof needed:** the accredited lab report. **Without it this slide is the weakest in the deck instead of the strongest.**

### 5 · Why now
Three converging forces: delivery is now the default consumption channel across South Asia and the Gulf; franchising is how food brands scale in these markets; and energy instability makes cold-chain-free formats structurally advantaged.

**Proof needed:** third-party market data, cited on-slide. **[CONFIRM]** — no number goes here uncited.

## Part 2 — It works (slides 6–9)

### 6 · Traction
Where the five years pay off. This is the slide that separates you from a pitch with a prototype.

- 15 trading sites, four operating models, three cities plus Singapore
- ~8,000 waffles a day
- Five years trading — first outlet Banani, 16 Dec 2021
- ~38,556 loyalty cards distributed Feb–May 2026
- 100+ team

Growth curve across the five years, one line, going up.

**Proof needed:** **reconciled** financials. This slide is blocked until `DILIGENCE.md` §1 closes. Do not put an unreconciled revenue figure on a slide — it will be tested, and being caught once ends the round.

### 7 · The business model
Three lines, and how they compound.

1. **Premix supply** — recurring B2B, high margin, exportable
2. **Franchise fees and royalties** — capital-light, franchisee-funded
3. **Owned outlets** — cash flow, brand control, and the lab where products are proven

State the shift plainly: *revenue mix moves from majority owned-outlet today toward majority premix-and-royalty over the plan.*

**Proof needed:** premix gross margin at scale; the franchise fee and royalty structure.

### 8 · The flywheel
One diagram, no bullet points.

> Premix removes the cold chain and guarantees consistency → franchising becomes cheap, fast and low-risk → franchisees open → premix volume rises → unit cost falls, R&D budget grows → product improves and range widens → franchising becomes more attractive.

Owned outlets in the centre: every product is proven there before it enters the system.

*Say the pattern out loud in the room:* the strongest food franchises make their money selling franchisees the proprietary mix, not on franchise fees. It relocates you out of the restaurant bucket in one sentence.

### 9 · Unit economics
Where "easily profitable" either becomes real or dies.

Per outlet model — quick-dispatch window, cafe, cart, cloud kitchen — set out capex, AOV, gross margin, monthly contribution and **payback period**. Then the franchisee's version of the same table, because that is what determines whether anyone buys one.

Then the number that reframes everything: **premix gross margin**, and what one franchisee is worth per year in premix volume alone.

**Proof needed:** all of it. Nothing on this slide may be estimated. See `DILIGENCE.md` §3.

## Part 3 — It scales (slides 10–13)

### 10 · Market
Bangladesh first — 170M people, a young median age, a fast-growing urban middle class. Then the expansion surface: South-East Asia and the GCC, reachable because **dry goods ship and cold chain does not.**

Sized honestly. A credible bottom-up number beats an inflated top-down one every time, and experienced investors discount top-down on sight.

**Proof needed:** cited third-party sources. **[CONFIRM]**

### 11 · Go-to-market — the franchise engine
How a franchisee is found, qualified, trained, opened and supplied. Site-to-opening timeline. The support package. Territory model.

The punchline: **because the premix guarantees the product, the franchisee only has to run a shop — not master a craft.**

**Proof needed:** the franchise pack must exist. See `DILIGENCE.md` §4.

### 12 · Competition and moat
Do not claim there is no competition — there is, and saying otherwise reads as naive.

Position against local dessert QSR, international franchise dessert brands, and cafés. The axes that matter: cold-chain dependency, consistency at scale, capex per unit, delivery durability.

The moat is a stack, not one thing: **formulation IP · the brand and ten owned characters · the documented franchise system · the loyalty engine · five years of operating data.** Say plainly which layer you are actually relying on.

**Proof needed:** IP filing status. An honest "trade secret plus system plus brand" beats an overstated patent claim that collapses in diligence.

### 13 · The 18-month plan
Six quarters, the milestones, and the metrics that unlock a Series A. Drawn from `18-MONTH-PLAN.md`.

Investors read this slide to answer one question: *does this founder know what the money is for?*

The allocation from `18-MONTH-PLAN.md` §4, as one chart plus one line of justification each.

Lead with the plant. **The largest line should be the thing that makes you not-a-restaurant.**

Close on the vision line, once, without decoration.

## Appendix — where diligence actually happens

Never presented; always sent. Half your investor questions get answered here without a second meeting.

1. Reconciled financials — three years, by outlet, by channel, by entity
2. Outlet-level P&L for all four models
3. Franchisee P&L model and the franchise pack
4. **The lab report** — texture and shelf-life testing on the crunch claim
5. Premix specification, cost build-up and margin at three volume tiers
6. IP position — filings, trade-secret regime, trademarks by market
7. Cap table and corporate structure, including the Banani entity
8. Cohort and repeat-purchase data, and the Surprise Card loyalty numbers
9. Delivery-platform performance by site
10. Awards, halal and food-safety certification
11. Press archive
12. Expansion pipeline — signed, in negotiation, exploring, labelled honestly

## Two versions, one source

**The send-ahead deck** — reads alone, more words, no presenter. This is what gets forwarded inside a fund, so it must survive being read by someone you never met.

**The room deck** — same order, far fewer words, images carry it. You are the narration.

Build the send-ahead version first; the room version is a subtraction from it.

## Failure modes to design against

| Trap | Why it kills |
|---|---|
| Leading with the chain, not the premix | You get valued as a restaurant. Everything else follows from this one mistake |
| An unreconciled revenue number | Tested within minutes. Being caught once ends the round |
| "Easily profitable" without the table | The claim that invites the question you cannot answer |
| A patent implied but not filed | Diligence finds it. It costs you trust on every other claim |
| Jester voice in the financials | Reads as unserious about money |
| Top-down market sizing | Discounted on sight by anyone experienced |
| Hiding the Banani entity | Found in diligence. Volunteered, it is housekeeping; discovered, it is a governance problem |
| No FX or repatriation answer | The first question from any foreign investor. Not having one ends the meeting |
