# Diligence — proof gaps and the data room

What an investor will ask for, what exists today, and what has to be built.

**Nothing in this repo is diligence-grade yet.** There is no financial, premix, franchise or IP material anywhere in the folder — I checked. Every section below starts from zero.

**The standing rule applies harder here than anywhere else:** never invent a price, an outlet, a date, an award or a statistic. In marketing an invented number is embarrassing. In a fundraise it is fraud, and it is discovered.

---

## 1. Financial reconciliation — the blocker

**Status: not started. Blocks slides 6, 7, 9, 14 and the entire appendix.**

There is currently no single source that produces a correct company revenue number. Three causes, all live:

| Cause | Effect |
|---|---|
| **GiantSoft** (8 standard outlets) and **ZAB** (4 Chef's Table) are different systems | Two definitions of a sale. ZAB's detail export duplicates item-line pages — aggregate naively and Chef's Table is overstated by **~29%** |
| **Foodpanda's portal is a channel report, not a POS** | Its GMV cannot be added to POS revenue. The same sale sits in both. Adding them double-counts |
| An **Otomatic** era sits between two GiantSoft eras, switch dates unrecorded | Any multi-year trend line silently compares two different definitions |

**Required:** three years, reconciled, **by outlet, by channel, by entity**, on one stated methodology, signed by an accountant. Plus monthly management accounts, a driver-based three-year projection, and the working file showing how each source was reconciled — investors ask for the working, not just the answer.

**This is the long pole. Start it first.** Everything else in the raise waits on it.

## 2. The premix — the asset you are actually selling

**Status: exists in the kitchen. Exists nowhere else.**

### IP position
- Formulation documented and dated, with authorship and a chain of custody
- Counsel's opinion: **patentable, or trade secret?** Many formulations are not patentable, and a patent publishes your recipe — sometimes trade secret is the stronger answer. Decide deliberately, not by default
- Priority filing where the answer is patent; a documented **trade-secret regime** where it is not — NDAs, access control, split-knowledge production, employment and contractor IP-assignment clauses
- Trademarks: the premix name and WaffleUp marks in every target market, including Dubai, Thailand and Indonesia
- Confirm all IP is owned by the **Singapore entity**, not by the founder personally and not by the BD opco

> **Do not disclose the formulation to any investor, lab or manufacturer before counsel has set the strategy and an NDA is signed.** A disclosure now can destroy patentability later.

### The crunch claim
"Crunchy for more than an hour" must become a number from an accredited lab.

- **Texture profile analysis** on an instrument, measured over time — 0, 15, 30, 60, 90 minutes
- **Benchmarked against competitors**, because "crunchy at 60 minutes" only means something next to what a normal waffle does
- Tested under real delivery conditions: in the actual box, at Dhaka ambient temperature and humidity
- Shelf-life and stability testing on the premix itself, plus nutritional and microbiological panels
- Allergen declaration and halal certification for the premix as a manufactured product

**Book the lab in month 1.** Turnaround is long and slide 4 is blocked until the report exists.

### Production
- Cost build-up: ingredients, packaging, labour, overhead, wastage
- Gross margin at three volume tiers
- Facility options — own plant versus contract manufacture — with capex, timeline and licensing for each
- Supply chain: ingredient sourcing, single-source dependencies, FX exposure on imports
- Batch coding, traceability and recall procedure

## 3. Unit economics

**Status: not built. This is where "easily profitable" is proven or lost.**

Per outlet model — quick-dispatch window, cafe, cart, cloud kitchen:

Capex to open · fit-out cost · AOV · covers or transactions per day · COGS % · labour % · rent % · other opex · monthly contribution · **payback period** · 24-month cohort performance by opening date.

Then the same table from the franchisee's side, because that is what determines whether anyone buys one: total investment, fee, royalty, premix cost, expected return, payback.

Then, by channel: dine-in, takeaway, own delivery and each aggregator — with **commission rates stated** (cloud-kitchen commission runs ~4 points higher). And the Chef's Table price premium of +16% to +33%, explained as a deliberate channel strategy rather than an inconsistency.

**A cohort view matters more than an average.** Investors want to know whether outlets opened in 2025 perform like those opened in 2022 — that is the question of whether the model is improving or decaying.

## 4. Franchise pack

**Status: does not exist. "Franchise ready" is currently a claim with nothing behind it.**

Operations manual · training curriculum and certification · site selection criteria · fit-out specification and approved suppliers · equipment list · the franchise agreement itself · fee and royalty structure · territory and exclusivity model · franchisee P&L model · support package · quality-control and audit regime · supply agreement for the premix · termination and transfer provisions.

Plus the pipeline: enquiries received to date, qualification criteria, and any LOIs or signed agreements. **Real franchise demand — even unconverted — is strong evidence.** If people have already asked to buy one, that is a slide.

## 5. Corporate, legal and cap table

**Status: partially known, one known problem.**

- Group structure: **Waffle Up Global PTE Ltd** (Singapore) and **WAFFLE UP LIMITED** (BD) — confirm the BD opco is a properly documented subsidiary
- **The Banani problem.** Banani trades under a separate entity and BIN — `CLOUD` / `003596326-0101` rather than `005242361-0101`. A related-party entity holding an operating asset outside the group is a genuine red flag. Resolve it, or document exactly why it exists. **Volunteer it early; discovered late it becomes a governance question rather than housekeeping**
- Cap table: current, fully diluted, with any options, loans, convertibles or informal promises. **Informal equity promises made verbally over five years are the most common cap-table landmine in a first institutional round** — surface them now
- Incorporation documents, registers, board minutes, shareholder agreements
- Registered addresses — `brand/BRAND.md` and the 2024 profile give two different Banani addresses. Resolve
- Trade licences, VAT registration, tax filings and clearances in both jurisdictions
- Leases for all 15 sites, with expiry and renewal terms
- Employment contracts for key staff, with IP assignment
- Supplier and delivery-platform agreements
- Any litigation, disputes or contingent liabilities
- Insurance
- **FX and repatriation:** the mechanism by which foreign investment enters and returns. Get counsel's written view before the first meeting

## 6. Commercial and operational

- Order volume, AOV and repeat rate by channel; delivery-platform performance by site
- **Surprise Card data** — 38,556 distributed Feb–May 2026, 2,839 redeemed Jan–Aug, 3–4% redemption with a 2–3× spread between best and worst outlet. This is real cohort data on a proprietary loyalty asset and it is more interesting to investors than you probably think
- Customer counts and any CRM data
- Social and content performance across the three channels
- Awards with issuing body and year; halal certificate, current and legible; food-safety licences
- Press archive, verified and dated
- Expansion pipeline labelled honestly: **signed**, **in negotiation**, **exploring**. "Coming soon" against a market with no signed agreement is exactly what a diligence reader checks
- **Retire the Egypt claim everywhere.** The 2024 profile says outlets in UAE and Egypt are soon to launch. Egypt is not in the pipeline. If both documents are in circulation, that contradiction is found

## 7. Data room structure

```
00-summary/          one-pager, deck, cap table summary
01-financials/       reconciled accounts, management accounts, projections, methodology
02-premix/           spec, cost model, lab reports, certifications, production plan
03-ip/               filings, trade-secret regime, trademarks by market
04-unit-economics/   outlet P&Ls, cohorts, channel analysis, franchisee model
05-franchise/        manual, agreement, training, pipeline
06-corporate/        structure, cap table, incorporation, leases, licences
07-commercial/       orders, loyalty, customers, platform performance
08-team/             bios, contracts, org chart, hiring plan
09-market/           sourced market data, competitive set
10-brand/            company profile, brand guidelines, press, awards
```

**Access-controlled, logged, watermarked.** Who opened what and for how long is real signal about who is actually interested.

Stage it: `00`, `01`, `04` at first serious interest; the rest post-term-sheet. Sending everything on day one signals desperation and gives away the premix before you are protected.

---

## 8. Rules for this workspace

1. **Never invent a number.** `[CONFIRM]` in a draft; never on a slide.
2. **Never let two documents disagree.** The profile, the deck and the data room draw from one set of verified facts.
3. **One methodology for revenue**, stated, applied everywhere, forever.
4. **Volunteer the weak points.** Every one of them is findable. Named first, they read as self-awareness; found later, as concealment.
5. **Protect the formulation before disclosing it** — to investors, labs and manufacturers alike.
6. **Diligence-grade, not publishable-grade.** The company profile needs facts good enough to print. This needs facts good enough to be audited.
