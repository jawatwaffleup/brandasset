Before you start

how to use this book

If a rule here and a file on the drive disagree, this book and the token files win. Everything is versioned together.

one library

Every mark, colour code, typeface, character and photograph shown in this book ships in the same portable kit. Use the supplied files. Do not rebuild artwork by hand, trace it, or generate a new version of it.

two builds

Screens get the RGB build. Print, packaging and signage get the CMYK build with the matching Pantone references. They are not interchangeable and they do not look identical — that is intentional.

two markets

Ten canonical characters, one universal roster — Merlulu included. The old Bangladesh-nine / Singapore-ten split was retired in August 2026. Market rules still bind pricing, outlets and delivery CTAs; they no longer bind the cast.

What this book covers

Identity, colour, typography, the pattern, the character system, voice, photography and video, packaging, event material and the loyalty programme artwork.

Where the files live

assets/ artwork  ·  brand/ written guidance  ·  tokens/ design tokens for software  ·  data/characters.json approved character copy  ·  preview.html the browsable catalogue.

The vision names procedures and processes, not just product. Improving how the company runs is vision work, not back-office overhead.

reimagine

Reimagine the consumer experience of eating waffles and fuse cuisines into products nobody else is making. Constant reimagination is what keeps growth exponential.

Redefining waffles as street food set the brand apart. To grow sustainably, keep redefining both the products and the procedures behind them.

Reimagine feeds the product line, redefine feeds the format, revolutionize feeds the process. A brand book is a process document: use it, and the identity survives every new outlet, market and campaign intact.

01 · The brand

the promise

One line the whole brand answers to, and the four pieces of the product that make it true rather than aspirational.

Who it’s for

A style-aware urban teenager-to-twentysomething with money in their pocket and a phone in their hand, who treats dessert as something to post, not something to plan.

What was wrong

Dessert was an occasion you had to travel to and eat at a table — and when it finally started travelling to you, it arrived soggy, melted and obviously “delivery-friendly”.

The promise

joy that
travels

The stick

A handle. Dessert you can walk, ride, queue and pose with. No table, no fork, no sitting down.

The square

Packs flat, stacks, doesn’t roll, doesn’t crush. Square is the new heart is an engineering claim before it is a tagline.

The box

It arrives as an object worth filming, not a sad container. The packaging is part of the product.

Late night

Joy travels when you want it, not when a restaurant is seating. No occasion required.

Never sell it as artisanal, indulgent, premium patisserie or “treat yourself” luxury. The brand exists to take the luxury off the waffle. That register is a hard fail, in copy and in art direction alike.

don’ts

Every one of these has happened. None of them is acceptable, including on internal decks and quick social posts.

× Cropped corners, uneven spacing
× Stretched or squashed
× Recoloured or greyscale

From the guideline

×  Uneven spacing and cropped-out corners
×  Solid coloured letters without the signature wave
×  Single coloured letters without the signature wave
×  The logo without the registered “®” symbol

And also never

digital & print

Three reference sheets ship with the kit. Hand the right one to the right supplier and the brand stays the same colour across every surface.

RGB — screens

Pantone — spot colour

Print tolerance

The specified codes must be held as strictly as the process allows. A small acceptable range of variation exists in print media; anything beyond it goes back to the supplier. Always supply the CMYK build together with the Pantone reference.

Legacy note

The 2022 Brand Book V.1 lists #6DCBD8 / #F0639A / #FFD66D / #3E1312. Those are superseded — treat any old-file colours you meet as the print/CMYK line, and rebuild digital work on the current RGB values. Some packaging artwork prints in a hotter magenta and mint green. That artwork is off-palette: never sample from it, never grade a photograph toward it.

Dominant · 60–75%

One colour owns the surface. Cyan or pink — never both at once.

Secondary · 15–25%

The counterpoint. If cyan is the field, pink is the counterpoint, and the other way round.

Accents · 3–5% each

At most two accents. Gold is the highlight, cocoa is the ink and the outline. White is the wave.

Rules

General Sans for anything a person reads at speed. Bebas Neue for large numerals, tickers and price labels. CHUM and Futura Extra Bold for headers, splash screens, printed receipts and staff-facing celebration moments — not for dense data.

Numerals

Use tabular figures for totals and counts so columns line up. Currency is BDT with no decimals: BDT 250 or ৳250.

Touch

Minimum touch target 48 px. Staff use these tools one-handed, mid-shift, sometimes with wet hands.

05 · The brand pattern

the signature wave

One artwork file, one rule: keep it behind the content, never at full contrast under body text.

Use it for

Splash and login screens · dashboard headers · printed receipt headers and footers · empty states behind a character · section dividers · packaging panels · event backdrops.

Keep it behind

Knock the pattern back or confine it to a band when text sits on top. The pattern is a field, not a texture to tile over everything.

Files

Two different jobs, both true

Bhoppu is the centre of the fiction — the character the stories are built around. Mr Waffle is the mascot and ambassador — the character who represents Waffle Up to the outside world, and the one on the packaging. Neither displaces the other.

Three movement languages

Bhoppu is mass. Air Maxi is motion. Mr Waffle is stillness. Everyone else sits between them. If two characters are moving the same way in one frame, one of them is wrong.

Movement is part of the identity, not a director’s choice. Two of these are powers the character has; the third is a hard limit.

Spacy — not grounded on Earth

On Earth, Spacy never touches the ground. She floats, drifts or flies. Feet planted means she is somewhere else entirely — another planet, an asteroid, a deck.

Mr Waffle — can float

Mr Waffle can float — it is a feature, not a limitation. Hovering, deliberate, small precise adjustments; R2-D2 is the reference. He also stands, walks and kicks a ball. Float him when it earns the shot.

Swirly — never speaks

Swirly never speaks aloud and never appears with a speech bubble. Sounds and reactions only. A written caption is allowed, and reads as thought.

Feet down means off-world

In the planet-spinning pose Spacy is standing — and that is the tell. Ground under Spacy is the reader’s cue that the scene has left Earth. Set it against sky, stars or an alien surface, never a Dhaka street.

And the rule underneath all three

Bhoppu is mass, Air Maxi is motion, Mr Waffle is stillness, and everyone else sits between them. If two characters are moving the same way in one frame, one of them is wrong.

07 · Voice & tone

the jester

Jester brands use humour and wit to engage. Puns, jokes and wordplay are explicitly part of the identity.

The whole company exists to take waffles down from the luxury dessert trolley and put them on a stick, on the street. The voice does the same job: never precious, never corporate.

funny, not silly

The joke never gets in the way of the information. If a reader has to decode the sentence to find the fact, rewrite it.

warm, not soft

Direct, quick, no hedging. Short sentences. No “please kindly be informed”, ever.

bangladeshi and global

Bangla and English mix freely — Ashol WaffleUp Chinun, Bangla Pizza, Square is the new heart.

07 · Voice & tone

two languages

Bangla is not a translation afterthought. Outlet teams may work in it primarily; management dashboards run in English.

Writing Bangla

Keep strings short — the display faces carry no Bangla, so set it in Noto Sans Bengali or Hind Siliguri. Humour translates badly: prefer warm-and-plain over pun-based. Numbers and currency stay in Western numerals with BDT or the taka sign.

Writing English

Sensory and a little over the top for product copy — “Death by Nutella… RIP.” Plain and quick for anything functional. Code-switching between the two languages is on-brand, not a mistake.

The authenticity line

Ashol WaffleUp Chinun — “know the real Waffle Up”. Used on menus, packaging and signage as the Bangla mark of the genuine article.

07 · Voice & tone

07 · Voice & tone

talking about value

The one rule in this section with no exceptions, in any channel, in any language. Confirmed August 2026.

Waffle Up may give value away. It must never name it, quantify it, or label it.

Never

×  The words “free” and “discount” — any copy, any channel, any language
×  Marking a menu price down. A 380 item is not sold at 280
×  “% off”, “save BDT X”, “was / now”, “deal”, “offer”, “cheap”, “bargain”
×  Countdown urgency pointed at a price drop
×  “On the house” — it still names the thing

Instead

✓  Combos and set pricing — Waffle Madness 1:4 at 720. The saving exists, unnamed
✓  Surprise Card scratch rewards — value arrives as luck, not as a markdown
✓  Gifts framed as earned, unlocked, won or surprising
✓  Scarcity of a thing — limited edition, season ending
✓  “Comes with”, “your reward”, “the surprise”, “collect”

Why it matters

Discount language makes Waffle Up feel cheap, and the whole brand is built on being street without being cheap. Those are different things. The scratch card exists precisely because it delivers value without starting a price conversation.

When in doubt

If the customer could work out the taka value from the words on screen, rewrite it.

10 · Merchandise

on an object

Merchandise is the brand at arm’s length from a shop. It has to be recognisable across a room and survive being sat on.

One field per item

Pick cyan or pink as the dominant colour of the object, the other as the counterpoint, gold as the highlight and cocoa as the ink. The 60–75 / 15–25 / 3–5 ratio applies to a flask exactly as it does to a screen.

One brand device

Every piece carries one of the four devices — the logo, the symbol, a word-mark or a character. Never a bare surface, and never all four fighting on the same face.

The pattern wraps

The signature wave is the default ground for anything that wraps — bags, cups, boxes, sock cuffs, balloon bodies. Keep it behind the mark, never at full contrast under type.

Small items use the symbol

Below the logo minimum — 120 px on screen, 25 mm in print — drop to the W monogram in its gold circle. Pop sockets, pin badges, sock cuffs, pen clips and zip pulls all take the symbol, not the lock-up.

Textile & single colour

Embroidery, debossing and one-colour print break thin cocoa keylines. Use the line-art character or the symbol, scale the outline weight up with the artwork, and never fake the four-layer type effect in thread.

Colour build

Everything physical is produced from the CMYK artwork with the Pantone references attached. Only the e-commerce photography and the listing graphics use the RGB build.

Approvals

New merchandise artwork goes back to the brand owner before production. Characters are never redrawn, re-posed, recoloured or AI-generated for a product — if the pose you need does not exist, request approved artwork.

11 · The Surprise Card

how it works

Two card types come out of the same envelope. Every card carries a unique serial.

Character card

Collectible. The character’s approved bio on the front, colour-in line art on the back, and an invitation to share the finished drawing. Ten designs.

Scratch card

Rarer. An instant win — a free item or a piece of merchandise — redeemable at designated outlets, with a printed validity date.

Earning

One card per BDT 500 spent, handed over at the till with the order.

Serials

Every card carries a unique serial, in the form FLL-9525656492. Batches are tracked centrally.

At the till

Issue and redemption are two separate zero-value categories. Any system touching the programme must keep that distinction — it is the only thing that makes it measurable.
