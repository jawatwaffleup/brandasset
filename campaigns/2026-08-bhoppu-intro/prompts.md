## The idea

Bhoppu's whole thing is **elevated spots**. His approved bio: *"a knack for finding those elevated spots where waffles seem extra flavourful… some say it's his natural zest for life; others think there's a secret ingredient."*

So the intro doesn't introduce him — **it introduces the spot.** The camera climbs, cresting a rooftop ledge, and he's already up there. The reveal is the view. That's the difference between Bhoppu and every other high-energy character in the cast: his joy has a destination.

**He does not move and he does not speak.** Only 2D artwork exists, and character voices are unapproved — so he sits in frame as a static cut-out while the camera does the work. The captions narrate in third person.

# STILLS ROUTE — recommended

**Why:** all three shots are camera moves over static scenes. Nothing in them genuinely animates. Generating stills and moving the camera in edit costs a fraction of the credits, gives higher resolution to scale into, and avoids AI video morphing the architecture — which is exactly where it fails worst on hard-light straight lines.

**Generate everything at the highest resolution the model offers** — plates get scaled up to 130%, and that's where low resolution shows.

**Model:** any photoreal model strong on architecture — Flux, Seedream, Imagen all work.

**C is wide on purpose.** You crop a 9:16 window out of it and slide that window sideways — that's the lateral drift, and it's also the walk shot. A vertical still has no room to travel horizontally.

## The edit moves

| Plate | Move | How |
|---|---|---|
| A | Crane up | Scale ~130%, animate vertical position upward over 2s |
| B | Slow push | Scale 100% → 110% over 4s |
| C | Drift / walk | Crop 9:16, slide the crop horizontally across the wide plate |

For real parallax on C, mask the near ledge off the skyline as two layers and slide the foreground faster. Ten minutes of work, and it's the difference between depth and a flat pan.

## Making Bhoppu "walk" without generating him

**The world moves, not the character.** Fix `hero.png` in frame, slide the background past, and add a small vertical bob — 2–4px at roughly two cycles per second, with a degree or two of rotation and a contact shadow bobbing in sync. That reads as walking. It's how cutout animation has always worked.

> ⚠ **Never feed `hero.png` into an image-to-video or "Reference" model to make him move.** That generates new poses from his artwork, which is redrawing him — same hard rule as generating him from scratch. Motion comes from transforming the *layer*, never from regenerating the art.
>
> Reference models are fine for the **environment** — once you have a rooftop plate you like, use it as the reference so the other plates match its light and skyline. Style consistency, never character.
